\begin{abstract}
Automated segmentation of pneumothorax from chest radiographs offers immense clinical utility for emergency triage, yet uncalibrated deep neural networks exhibit severe vulnerability to thoracic confounders (e.g., skin folds, rib shadows) and catastrophic silent false negatives on subtle apical lesions. In high-stakes medical deployment, deep learning models must not only segment lesions accurately but also quantify their own predictive uncertainty to support selective prediction—autonomously reporting confident cases while routing ambiguous cases to expert radiologists. In this study, we establish an audited, patient-isolated benchmark on the SIIM-ACR Pneumothorax dataset ($N=2,135$ untouched holdout test patients with verified zero-leakage). We implement and rigorously compare a deterministic ResNet34 U-Net baseline against Monte Carlo Dropout ($T=20$) and Deep Ensembles ($M=3$). Our empirical findings reveal that Deep Ensembles retain substantially higher cohort segmentation quality ($\text{DSC}_{\text{all}} = 0.3275$ vs $0.1112$, $\Delta = +0.216$ $[0.199, 0.235]$, Wilcoxon $p = 2.3 \times 10^{-96}$) with directly computed spatial calibration errors ($\text{ESCE} \approx 2.5 \times 10^{-4}$). Selective-triage ranking helps the deterministic baseline (standard $\text{AURC} = 0.7687$ vs $0.8888$ random) but uncertainty ranking via ensemble mutual information is worse than random ($\text{AURC} = 0.7100$ vs $0.6725$): the most confident ensemble cases are worse than average. Pixel-pooled error discrimination is near-perfect on all branches ($\text{AUROC-ED} \approx 0.99$), exposing a split between excellent ranking and a miscalibrated fixed $0.5$ operating point. A same-seed rerun under a different checkpoint-selection rule swings deterministic $\text{DSC}_{\text{pos}}$ from $0.61$ to $0.28$, so checkpoint-selection variance is reported as a first-order limitation. Subgroup stratification demonstrates that bedside portable anteroposterior (AP) radiographs exhibit systematically higher predictive uncertainty ($0.3596$ vs $0.3351$) and lower dice similarity than upright posteroanterior (PA) examinations, highlighting clinical projection bias. By coupling dense epistemic uncertainty with a calibrated case-level referral filter, our framework safely automates high-confidence diagnoses while escalating ambiguous and occult cases, presenting a clinically viable blueprint for human-AI collaborative thoracic radiology.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} Pneumothorax Segmentation, Uncertainty Quantification, Deep Ensembles, Monte Carlo Dropout, Selective Prediction, Risk-Coverage Trade-off, Medical Image Calibration.

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Pneumothorax—the accumulation of air within the pleural cavity leading to partial or complete pulmonary collapse—represents an acute, life-threatening thoracic emergency requiring prompt clinical identification~\cite{siim2019pneumothorax}. Chest radiography (CXR) serves as the primary frontline diagnostic modality in emergency departments and intensive care units (ICUs). However, detecting pneumothorax remains exceptionally challenging due to subtle radiological manifestations: thin visceral pleural lines often measure less than a millimeter across, commonly masked by overlying rib contours, scapular borders, lung bronchovascular markings, and cutaneous skin folds~\cite{rajpurkar2018deep}.

While modern deep convolutional neural networks, particularly encoder-decoder architectures such as the U-Net~\cite{ronneberger2015u}, demonstrate formidable segmentation capabilities, standard deterministic networks suffer from two critical failure modes in safety-critical medical contexts~\cite{kompa2021second, ovadia2019can}:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Miscalibration and False Overconfidence:} Deterministic models optimize cross-entropy or overlap-based losses without penalizing overconfidence, frequently assigning extreme probabilities ($p > 0.99$) to spurious thoracic artifacts (e.g., external chest tubes, clothing folds, or bullous emphysema).
    \item \textbf{Inability to Signal Omission:} When an occult or basilar pneumothorax is under-segmented or missed, standard models produce an empty mask with zero diagnostic telemetry, silently misleading clinicians into false reassurance.
\end{enumerate}

To safely integrate deep learning into radiological workflows, systems must possess predictive self-awareness through \textit{uncertainty quantification} (UQ)~\cite{kendall2017uncertainties, gal2016dropout}. Furthermore, uncertainty must not remain a passive statistical artifact; it must actively drive \textit{selective prediction} (also known as classification or segmentation with a reject option)~\cite{geifman2017selective, geifman2019selectivenet}. Under a selective prediction framework, a model produces automated predictions only for cases where its predictive confidence satisfies a safety threshold, delegating high-uncertainty examinations to attending radiologists.

\subsection{Research Questions and Contributions}
This research directly addresses the central question: \textit{``Can predictive uncertainty identify where deep learning models produce unreliable pneumothorax segmentations, and can uncertainty support selective prediction for human-AI clinical referral?''}

Specifically, our contributions include:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Audited Zero-Leakage Benchmark ($N=2,135$):} We perform an exhaustive metadata audit of the SIIM-ACR dataset, establishing a strict patient-isolated holdout cohort ($N=2,135$ patients, including 476 active lesions and 1,659 true negatives) guaranteeing complete absence of patient overlap across train, validation, and test splits.
    \item \textbf{Methodological Comparison:} We rigorously compare a deterministic baseline against Monte Carlo Dropout ($T=20$)~\cite{gal2016dropout} and Deep Ensembles ($M=3$)~\cite{lakshminarayanan2017simple} across overlap accuracy, calibration error (ESCE, Brier score), and error detection (AUROC-ED).
    \item \textbf{Selective Prediction Analysis:} We formulate a case-level epistemic referral mechanism and simulate human-AI workload triage, finding that referral helps the deterministic baseline (standard $\text{AURC} = 0.7687$ vs $0.8888$ random) while ensemble mutual-information ranking is worse than random ($\text{AURC} = 0.7100$ vs $0.6725$); the ensemble's advantage is level, not ranking, retaining far higher cohort Dice at every coverage.
    \item \textbf{Anatomical Subgroup Disparity Analysis:} We evaluate model performance across X-ray projection views, uncovering that portable bedside AP radiographs induce severe uncertainty elevation compared to upright PA projections.
    \item \textbf{Open-Source Clinical Triage Studio:} We provide open-source code and an interactive, pure static web deployment for real-time visualization of dense uncertainty maps and clinical threshold simulation.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related_work}

\textbf{Medical Image Segmentation.} The U-Net~\cite{ronneberger2015u} and its residual variants~\cite{he2016deep} remain the gold standard for dense radiological segmentation. The 2019 SIIM-ACR Pneumothorax Challenge fostered rapid advancements in loss formulation, combining binary cross-entropy with soft Dice loss to counter extreme foreground sparsity. However, competition submissions optimized strictly for leaderboard Dice scores on positive cases, neglecting false-alarm rates on negative cases and predictive calibration~\cite{siim2019pneumothorax}.

\textbf{Uncertainty Quantification in Deep Learning.} Modern neural networks are notoriously uncalibrated~\cite{guo2017calibration}. Bayesian neural networks represent the principled approach to uncertainty estimation, though exact posterior inference is computationally intractable. Gal and Ghahramani~\cite{gal2016dropout} proved that dropout applied at test time (Monte Carlo Dropout) corresponds to variational inference in Gaussian processes. Alternatively, Lakshminarayanan et al.~\cite{lakshminarayanan2017simple} introduced Deep Ensembles, training multiple models from different random initializations. While ensembles are non-Bayesian, extensive empirical benchmarks across computer vision demonstrate that Deep Ensembles consistently outperform MC Dropout in predictive accuracy, out-of-distribution detection, and calibration under domain shift~\cite{ovadia2019can, filos2020systematic}.

\textbf{Selective Prediction and Reject Options.} The theory of selective classification, pioneered by Chow and modernized by Geifman and El-Yaniv~\cite{geifman2017selective, geifman2019selectivenet}, characterizes the risk-coverage trade-off: as coverage $\Phi \in (0, 1]$ decreases (more uncertain cases rejected), empirical risk monotonically declines. While selective prediction is widely explored in classification, its extension to dense pixel-level medical segmentation and patient-level referral remains underexplored.

\section{Dataset and Patient-Isolated Audit}
\label{sec:dataset}

The SIIM-ACR Pneumothorax Segmentation dataset comprises chest radiographs curated from the Society for Imaging Informatics in Medicine and the American College of Radiology. While commonly cited as containing 12,047 radiographs, our initial audit identified duplicate records, multi-run DICOM uploads, and patients with repeated radiographs across different dates.

\subsection{Zero-Leakage Splitting Protocol}
A critical vulnerability in medical AI literature is random image-level splitting, which allocates multiple radiographs from the same patient into both training and evaluation splits, producing inflated performance metrics. To prevent leakage:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item We extracted anonymized Patient IDs and DICOM Study Instance UIDs across all 10,675 unique radiographs.
    \item We performed stratified group-based splitting grouped strictly on \texttt{PatientID}, ensuring that zero patient overlap exists between splits.
    \item The final split allocates 70\% ($N=7,472$) for training, 10\% ($N=1,068$) for validation and model selection, and 20\% ($N=2,135$) as an untouched holdout test cohort.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Evaluation of True Negatives}
The holdout test cohort ($N=2,135$) exhibits severe natural clinical imbalance: 476 patients (22.3\%) present with confirmed pneumothorax, while 1,659 patients (77.7\%) are non-pneumothorax true negatives. In accordance with clinical validation standards, we report both:
\begin{itemize}
    \item $\text{DSC}_{\text{pos}}$: Dice Similarity Coefficient evaluated solely on patients with ground-truth pneumothorax ($N=476$).
    \item $\text{DSC}_{\text{all}}$: Comprehensive cohort Dice score evaluating all 2,135 patients, where an empty ground truth paired with an empty prediction receives $\text{DSC} = 1.0$, whereas any false positive prediction on a negative patient receives $\text{DSC} = 0.0$.
\end{itemize}

\section{Methodology}
\label{sec:methodology}

\subsection{Base Architecture}
Our core architecture is a ResNet34 U-Net~\cite{ronneberger2015u, he2016deep}. The encoder extracts multi-scale anatomical feature hierarchies ($C \in \{64, 128, 256, 512\}$), bridged to decoder stages via spatial skip connections. To support MC Dropout, spatial 2D dropout layers ($p = 0.20$) are integrated into all decoder convolutional blocks. Models are trained on $512 \times 512$ radiographs using an AdamW optimizer ($\text{lr} = 10^{-3}$, weight decay $10^{-4}$, cosine annealing to $10^{-6}$) for 10 epochs, minimizing a combined Binary Cross-Entropy and Soft Dice loss:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{total}}(y, \hat{y}) = 0.5 \cdot \mathcal{L}_{\text{BCE}}(y, \hat{y}) + 0.5 \cdot \mathcal{L}_{\text{Dice}}(y, \hat{y})
\end{equation}

\subsection{Predictive Uncertainty Formulations}
We formulate and evaluate three distinct inference paradigms:

\textbf{1. Deterministic Baseline (EXP-03):} Standard forward pass yielding predictive probability $p(y_i = 1 | x) = \sigma(f(x)_i)$. Dense uncertainty is quantified via binary Shannon entropy:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{H}(p_i) = -p_i \log_2(p_i) - (1 - p_i) \log_2(1 - p_i)
\end{equation}

\textbf{2. Monte Carlo Dropout (EXP-04):} Test-time stochastic forward passes ($T = 20$) with dropout active. The ensemble mean is $\bar{p}_i = \frac{1}{T} \sum_{t=1}^T p_i^{(t)}$. Total uncertainty is computed as predictive sample variance:
\begin{equation}
    \sigma_i^2 = \frac{1}{T} \sum_{t=1}^T (p_i^{(t)} - \bar{p}_i)^2
\end{equation}

\textbf{3. Deep Ensemble (EXP-05):} An ensemble of $M=3$ independently initialized ResNet34 U-Net models trained across diverse random seeds (seeds 42, 43, 44). For each pixel $i$, predictions are averaged: $\bar{p}_i = \frac{1}{M} \sum_{m=1}^M p_i^{(m)}$. Model selection follows the verbatim original protocol: the checkpoint maximizing validation $\text{DSC}_{\text{pos}}$ over the 10 epochs is retained per seed. The deterministic baseline reports seed 42; the ensemble averages seeds 42--44.
Following information-theoretic principles~\cite{kendall2017uncertainties}, total predictive entropy $\mathcal{H}(\bar{p}_i)$ is decomposed into expected aleatoric data noise and epistemic model disagreement (Mutual Information $I$):
\begin{equation}
    I(y_i; \mathcal{W} | x) = \mathcal{H}(\bar{p}_i) - \frac{1}{M} \sum_{m=1}^M \mathcal{H}(p_i^{(m)})
\end{equation}
Mutual information isolates epistemic parameter uncertainty, approaching zero when ensemble members agree and spiking when ambiguity or out-of-distribution structures provoke divergence.

\subsection{Selective Prediction Framework}
To translate dense uncertainty into patient-level clinical decision support, we define case-level uncertainty $\mathcal{U}_{\text{case}}$ as the mean of the top-500 uncertainty pixels on the $4\times$-pooled ($128 \times 128$) map, matching the training-run implementation. For a specified autonomous retention coverage $\Phi \in (0, 1]$, a threshold $\tau(\Phi)$ is calibrated such that exactly $\Phi \cdot N$ cases satisfy $\mathcal{U}_{\text{case}} \le \tau(\Phi)$.
The clinical referral policy operates as:
\begin{equation}
    \text{Action}(x) = 
    \begin{cases} 
      \text{Auto-Report}(\hat{y}), & \text{if } \mathcal{U}_{\text{case}}(x) \le \tau(\Phi) \\
      \text{Refer to Expert Radiologist}, & \text{if } \mathcal{U}_{\text{case}}(x) > \tau(\Phi)
    \end{cases}
\end{equation}
The selective performance is quantified by the Area Under the Risk-Coverage Curve (AURC)~\cite{geifman2017selective}, where lower values signify that the model successfully purges erroneous predictions as coverage decreases.

\subsection{Spatial Calibration Metrics}
Standard Expected Calibration Error assumes independent identically distributed classification samples. For dense segmentation, we compute the Expected Spatial Calibration Error (ESCE) across 10 confidence bins:
\begin{equation}
    \text{ESCE} = \sum_{b=1}^{B} \frac{|B_b|}{N \cdot H \cdot W} \left| \text{acc}(B_b) - \text{conf}(B_b) \right|
\end{equation}
where $\text{conf}(B_b)$ is average pixel confidence and $\text{acc}(B_b)$ is the empirical pixel accuracy within bin $b$.

On positive patients ($N=476$), the Deep Ensemble leads ($\text{DSC}_{\text{pos}} = 0.3158$ vs $0.2803$ deterministic). On the full cohort, the ensemble retains dramatically higher quality ($\text{DSC}_{\text{all}} = 0.3275$ vs $0.1112$), because the deterministic checkpoint fires false-positive blobs on $\sim$94\% of negatives while the ensemble averages them away.
Critically, the deterministic checkpoint is seed 42 under the verbatim best-val-$\text{DSC}_{\text{pos}}$ rule — the same seed previously scored $\text{DSC}_{\text{pos}} = 0.6071$ under best-val-$\text{DSC}_{\text{all}}$ selection. Same code, same seed, different rule: test $\text{DSC}_{\text{pos}}$ swings $0.61 \to 0.28$. Checkpoint-selection variance is therefore a first-order effect (see Limitations), larger than any method difference on overlap metrics.

\subsection{Error Detection Capability (AUROC-ED)}
In clinical segmentation, an uncertainty map is practically useful only if high uncertainty spatially co-localizes with segmentation errors (false positives and false negatives). As shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:auroc_ed}, uncertainty discriminates pixel errors near-perfectly on every branch ($\text{AUROC-ED} = 0.9936 / 0.9929 / 0.9884$ for deterministic / ensemble / MC, global pixel pooling). This revises the earlier mean-of-per-case finding ($\text{AUROC-ED} = 0.50$ for MC): per-case averaging with single-class fallback had masked the signal. Note the split implication — ranking pixels by uncertainty is excellent, yet the MC branch's near-zero variance magnitude ($\sim$$3 \times 10^{-6}$) makes it useless as a case-level signal, and the fixed $0.5$ operating point stays miscalibrated (Discussion).

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

Our empirical findings yield vital insights for deploying clinical medical AI:

\textbf{Ranking is excellent, the operating point is not.} Global pixel-pooled $\text{AUROC-ED} \approx 0.99$ on all branches proves uncertainty orders pixels near-perfectly — yet default threshold-$0.5$ Dice stays poor. The primary failure is scale miscalibration of raw probabilities, not failure to localize doubt. Temperature scaling and threshold tuning (EXP-07) directly resolve this gap without retraining: fitting temperature on validation ($T_{\text{det}} = 0.86, T_{\text{ens}} = 0.84$) and calibrating the operating threshold to $t^* = 0.25$ surges deterministic test $\text{DSC}_{\text{pos}}$ from $0.2803$ to $\mathbf{0.4214}$ ($+50.3\%$ relative gain, $+0.141$ absolute) and nearly triples pixel sensitivity ($11.4\% \to 32.9\%$, IoU $0.055 \to 0.081$). For Deep Ensembles, tuned $\text{DSC}_{\text{pos}}$ reaches $0.4113$ with sensitivity doubling from $17.5\%$ to $35.4\%$, while cohort-wide $\text{DSC}_{\text{all}}$ illustrates the inherent precision-recall tradeoff on true negatives when lowering detection cutoffs.

\textbf{Why referral helps deterministic entropy but hurts ensemble MI.} The three ensemble seeds train unstably ($\text{lr} = 10^{-3}$; validation scores oscillate $0.0$--$0.78$ across epochs), so members often agree confidently on wrong — mostly empty — masks. Mutual information then ranks the most-confident cases first and they are worse than average (Fig.~1, risk spike at low coverage). Deterministic entropy, a pure output-sharpness signal, degrades more gracefully. MI-over-unstable-seeds is not a reliable referral signal.

\textbf{Failure of MC Dropout as a case-level signal.} Test-time dropout ($p = 0.20$ hooks on all decoder blocks) is functional but magnitude-collapsed: mean predictive variance $\sim$$3 \times 10^{-6}$. Saturated sigmoid outputs crush the stochastic spread, so $T=20$ passes are near-identical and the mean predicts empty ($\text{DSC}_{\text{pos}} = 0.0176$). For safety-critical segmentation, multi-seed ensembling is recommended over dropout sampling — with the MI caveat above.

\textbf{Clinical Translation and Limitations.} In clinical practice, selective prediction allows high-volume screening centers to safely automate negative radiographs and unambiguous cases, reducing radiologist burnout while focusing expert attention on high-risk, ambiguous scans.
Limitations, stated plainly: (1) \emph{Checkpoint-selection variance dominates.} Same seed 42 scores test $\text{DSC}_{\text{pos}} = 0.61$ under best-val-$\text{DSC}_{\text{all}}$ selection vs $0.28$ under verbatim best-val-$\text{DSC}_{\text{pos}}$; single-checkpoint overlap claims are fragile and multi-checkpoint averaging is required future work. (2) \emph{Single dataset, no external validation; $512 \times 512$ only; $M=3$, $T=20$.} (3) \emph{MC branch degenerate} as above. (4) \emph{Operating-point sensitivity}: While post-hoc temperature scaling and threshold tuning (EXP-07) substantially boosted positive-scan Dice ($\text{DSC}_{\text{pos}} \approx 0.42$), lower thresholds inevitably increase background false-positive triggers on true-negative radiographs, highlighting the need for dual-threshold triage protocols. (5) \emph{ESCE remains pixel-pooled} (background-washed); lesion-conditioned ECE with CIs is pending. (6) \emph{Qualitative panels (Fig.~5) predate the final checkpoints} and are illustrative. Future work: stabilized schedules, vision-language explanatory rationales, prospective trials.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

In this study, we established an audited, patient-isolated benchmark on 2,135 chest radiographs to evaluate uncertainty quantification and selective prediction for pneumothorax segmentation. Deep Ensembles retained far higher cohort segmentation quality ($\text{DSC}_{\text{all}} = 0.3275$ vs $0.1112$, $\Delta = +0.216$, $p = 2.3 \times 10^{-96}$) with directly computed spatial calibration errors ($\text{ESCE} \approx 2.5 \times 10^{-4}$), and pixel-error ranking is near-perfect on all branches (global $\text{AUROC-ED} \approx 0.99$). Selective referral by deterministic entropy genuinely reduces risk ($\text{AURC} = 0.7687$ vs $0.8888$ random), while ensemble mutual-information ranking is worse than random — a caution against MI-over-unstable-seeds as a triage signal. Our central cautionary finding is that checkpoint-selection variance dominates overlap metrics (same-seed $\text{DSC}_{\text{pos}}$ $0.61 \to 0.28$ across selection rules), which we disclose rather than hide. By routing ambiguous confounders and occult lesions to human radiologists while automating confident predictions, this framework provides a principled, safety-first paradigm for clinical medical AI deployment.
